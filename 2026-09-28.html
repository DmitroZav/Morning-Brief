☀️ Big Story of the Day
Toronto just went from the world's strongest real estate market to one of its weakest — in four years

UBS, one of the world's biggest banks, publishes a "Global Real Estate Bubble Index" every year ranking major cities by how overheated or how weak their housing markets are. The 2026 edition (released Sept 22, widely reported across Canadian outlets Sept 27) puts Toronto and Vancouver among the weakest housing markets on the planet — a stunning reversal from 2022, when Toronto was ranked the single strongest market in the world (UBS, CTV News).

The numbers: Toronto home prices are down roughly 30% since their 2022 peak, adjusted for inflation, and Vancouver is down about 20%. Both cities are now rated "moderate" bubble risk instead of "elevated." UBS economist Matthias Holzhey says "higher-for-longer financing costs are likely to cap house-price gains in the near term," while CMHC's own deputy chief economist, Aled ab Iorwerth, points to the deeper cause: years of runaway price growth made both cities "very unaffordable," so when people started relocating to cheaper metros like Ottawa and Montreal after the pandemic, demand pulled back hard (Yahoo Finance / Bloomberg).

Why it's video-worthy: "world's strongest market to one of the weakest in 4 years" is a jaw-dropping stat even for someone who's never looked at a housing chart, and it's the single cleanest explanation yet for why the GTA market has felt so quiet all year — it also gives Dmytro's own "I bought near the peak" story a headline to hang on.

🎯 The One Thing
New government data: renters who move to a new place pay $450 MORE per month than renters who stay put — a $5,400/year "moving penalty."

Statistics Canada and CMHC released results from the Canadian Housing Survey (published Sept 23) comparing renters who recently moved to renters who've stayed in the same unit. Recent movers pay $1,740/month on average versus $1,290/month for people who stayed — a $450 monthly gap (STOREYS). Forty percent of recent movers are now in "unaffordable" housing (spending 30%+ of income on rent) versus 32% of people who didn't move. And 44.5% of recent movers say rising rent has caused them real financial difficulty, versus 33.5% of non-movers — that gap has widened 27 percentage points since 2018. This lands directly on newcomers, since moving to a new rental is exactly what someone arriving in Canada has to do.

🧠 Why It Matters (To You)

If you're a buyer or a first-timer: a market that went from "world's strongest" to "one of the weakest" in four years, plus condos flooding onto the market, means real negotiating room that simply didn't exist a few years ago — this is closer to a buyer's market than most people believe.

If you're a renter, and especially if you just moved here: the data confirms what you probably already feel in your bank account — moving costs you real money every month, not just the moving truck. Knowing that going in helps you negotiate harder before you sign a new lease, or think twice about moving just to save a small amount.

If you're a condo owner or thinking about buying one: today's oversupply story is a warning to check the numbers carefully in any building with a lot of investor units, but it can also mean more room to negotiate a better price than you'd get on a freehold home right now.

☀️ Big Story of the Day
Trump and Zelensky just sat down at the UN for 40 minutes — and Trump says "something is going to happen."

On Tuesday at the UN General Assembly in New York, President Trump and President Zelensky held a bilateral meeting focused on ending Russia's war on Ukraine, now in its fifth year — covering a possible ceasefire on energy-infrastructure attacks, more Patriot missile defence, and a new drone-cooperation agreement (Jerusalem Post). Afterward, Zelensky said Ukraine is ready for an energy ceasefire and called on Trump to arrange a trilateral meeting with Putin (CNBC, Al Jazeera). Trump wouldn't say what's in a deal but told reporters: "I think they're going to reach a deal... I think something is going to happen."

Why it's video-worthy: nothing is signed and no Putin meeting is on the calendar yet, but this is the most concrete Trump has sounded about an actual endpoint in months — and it matters to anyone with family still living through this war, whether or not they follow Canadian politics.

🎯 The One Thing
The Governor of the Bank of Canada just said out loud he's more worried about hiking rates than cutting them.

In a speech Monday in Halifax titled "Navigating Uncertainty and Adapting to Change," Tiff Macklem said he expects inflation to drift higher in the coming months — partly because oil near $100/barrel from the Middle East conflict keeps feeding gas prices — and warned policymakers "don't want to be late" raising rates if that turns out to be stubborn (Bank of Canada, Canadian Mortgage Trends). In the same speech he warned that if U.S. tariffs stay in place, Canada's growth could get roughly cut in half next quarter (BNN Bloomberg) — the market is now pricing in as many as 5 rate hikes, though BMO itself thinks that's overdone (Better Dwelling / BMO).

☀️ Big Story of the Day
A $130,000 tax break just split the housing market in two — new detached homes are flying, condos are frozen.

Ontario axed the full 13% HST — the sales tax — on eligible new homes on April 1, and the first full quarter of numbers is in. New-home sales across the province jumped 130% in Q2, to 8,410 units from 3,645 a year earlier. But it's really two markets: new single-family (detached) sales more than doubled, 2,582 to 7,215, while new condo sales barely moved — up just 12% to 1,195, still 88% below their 10-year average (Globe and Mail; The Hub).

The rebate knocks up to $130,000 off a new home priced up to $1.5 million, and runs until March 31, 2027 (CTV News). Real money — but clearly moving detached buyers, not condo buyers, because condo prices, fees and investor math still don't work.

Why it's video-worthy: "the government will hand you up to $130k" is a scroll-stopper, and almost nobody has explained who qualifies or why condos got left behind. That's idea 1.

🎯 The One Thing
Ontario's HST rebate is a real up-to-$130k discount on a new build up to $1.5M — but only on new construction (mostly detached). Resale homes and most condos get nothing.

Plain version: buying a brand-new detached, semi or town from a builder could save you tens of thousands. Buying resale or a condo? It doesn't apply — so "HST rebate" doesn't mean every home got cheaper. Sources: Globe; TRREB HST rebate PDF.

🧠 Why It Matters (To You)

First-time buyer eyeing a new build: the HST rebate is big — up to $130k off a new home up to $1.5M — but only on new construction, which is why new detached sales doubled. Resale and most condos don't qualify. Know your bucket before assuming a discount (Globe). Idea 1.

Why homes stay expensive in a "soft" market: Ontario promised 1.5M homes, but parts of the GTA — Pickering, Ajax, Whitby — literally can't plug new developments into the power grid, and fixing it could cost up to $120B nobody wants to pay for. Tight supply keeps prices up (Globe). Idea 2.

Buyer worried about overpaying: in a buyer's market you have more power than you think. A GTA agent just listed 10 reasons buyers are walking away — bad layouts, deferred repairs, overpricing. You're allowed to say no. Idea 3.

Nine: it's sat for weeks and the seller won't budge — leverage is yours. Ten: it doesn't fit your five-year life and you're stretching. Falling in love is fine, but you're allowed to walk.

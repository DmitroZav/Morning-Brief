☀️ Big Story of the Day
The 35% tariff went live — and the sky didn't fall

Everyone braced for chaos. On Friday the US officially raised its tariff — a tax on imported goods — on Canadian products that don't qualify under our free-trade deal (CUSMA) from 25% to 35%. Then the weekend arrived and… not much happened. Canada's main stock market (the TSX) actually ticked up 0.2% on Friday, the Canadian dollar barely moved (down about 0.2%), and bets on the Bank of Canada's next move didn't budge.

The calm has a simple reason: most of what Canada sells to the US still moves under CUSMA, so it's exempt. Prime Minister Mark Carney called the hike "disappointing" but pointed out Canada still faces among the lowest average tariff rates of any US trading partner, and said Ottawa has a "full range" of responses ready. The real thing to circle is August 19: a second wave of 50% tariffs on roughly $20 billion of goods — dairy, alcohol, autos, cement, lumber, furniture, even honey and hockey equipment.

Why it's video-worthy: the headlines screamed "35% TARIFF" and braced everyone for disaster. You get to be the calm friend who says: it happened, the market shrugged, here's the one date and the one thing that actually touch your wallet. Being the person who lowers the temperature is its own kind of viral.

🎯 The One Thing
Your fixed mortgage rate is creeping up even though the Bank of Canada hasn't moved — because the bond market, not the Bank, sets fixed rates.

The Bank of Canada has sat at 2.25% since last October. Yet the best 5-year fixed mortgage rates have quietly climbed back over 4% (roughly 4.0%–4.6% depending on the lender). The reason confuses almost every first-time buyer: a FIXED rate follows the government bond market, not the Bank. The 5-year Government of Canada bond yield is back up near 3.2% on higher oil prices and sticky inflation, and fixed rates rode up with it. Translation for you: if your plan was "wait for the Bank to cut and mortgages get cheaper," that plan isn't working — the part of the market that controls your fixed rate is going the other way.

🧠 Why It Matters (To You)

If you're a first-time buyer or newcomer: two things this weekend point the same way. Fixed rates are back over 4% and the Bank of Canada isn't going to rescue you with a quick cut — the economy is growing again. But the condo door is open wider than it's been in a while: GTA condo sales jumped 14.3% from last year, prices are still below 2024, and the expanded HST rebate can save real money on a new build. Your leverage is on price and choice today, not on a rate that keeps not falling.

If you're a renter: the tariff headline didn't change your rent this weekend, but the chain matters. If the Aug 19 wave taxes lumber and cement, building gets pricier, fewer homes get built, and tighter supply eventually nudges rents up. For now the condo glut still works in your favour — landlords are competing for tenants. If your lease is up, shop and negotiate instead of auto-renewing.

If you already own or you're renewing: fixed rates ticking over 4% is your nudge. If you renew in the next few months, ask your lender for a rate hold now — most lock today's number free for 90–120 days. It's free insurance against the next uptick. And if you're eyeing a pre-construction condo, read idea 3 before you sign anything — some buyers are now being sued over deals they walked away from.

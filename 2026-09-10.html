☀️ Big Story of the Day
President Zelenskyy just landed in Canada — and this week, he's coming to Toronto.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and First Lady Olena Zelenska arrived in Calgary Wednesday night for a three-day visit, meeting Prime Minister Mark Carney in Calgary this morning before addressing the full Canadian Cabinet at a planning forum in Banff (PM's Office statement). From there, the President and First Lady head to Toronto and North Bay before leaving Friday. The visit's official focus is deepening defence, economic, and energy cooperation between Canada and Ukraine — Canada has pledged $6.5 billion in military assistance for Ukraine since 2022, running through 2029 (CP24/CTV News). It's Zelenskyy's first visit to Canada this year; his last stop was Halifax back in December.

Why it's video-worthy: this isn't a headline that stays on the news channel — the President of Ukraine is going to physically stand in Toronto, in a city with one of the largest Ukrainian communities outside Ukraine. For a huge part of this audience, that's personal in a way a policy story never is.

🎯 The One Thing
Gas just hit its highest point since May — and it has nothing to do with Canada's own economy.

Brent crude, one of the two benchmark oil prices the world watches, closed above $101.50 a barrel on Tuesday — the first time it's been that high since May (CNBC). The cause: the US-Iran conflict is escalating again — Iran reportedly attempted a second, previously undisclosed attack on US Navy ships, and Iran-backed Houthi fighters struck energy facilities in Saudi Arabia. Even though Canada produces plenty of its own oil, our gas prices are still priced off these same global benchmarks. CAA's daily tracker puts today's national average at 177.2 cents a litre — up from 140.9 cents a year ago, a 26% jump, and up from 162.9 cents just a month ago (CAA National Gas Prices). President Trump has already said he doesn't expect relief before the US midterm elections in November.

Script

Hook: "If you're trying to get permanent residency through a job offer in Ontario, there's a step almost nobody explains — and it's not even on you, it's on your employer."

Body: "This came up in a video I saw this week, and it lines up with what I've seen with clients too. For a lot of employer-based PR streams through the Ontario Immigrant Nominee Program, your employer needs to be registered and, in some cases, go through a Labour Market Impact Assessment — an LMIA — before your nomination can even move forward. A lot of newcomers assume a signed job offer is the finish line. It's actually the starting line. If your employer has never sponsored someone before, this can take weeks they didn't plan for, and it can quietly stall your whole application while you're wondering why nothing's moving. When I moved here, I learned the hard way that immigration paperwork almost never depends only on you — half of it depends on people who've never done this before either. If you're job hunting for PR purposes, ask directly in the interview: has your company sponsored someone for PR before? It's not a rude question. It's the most important one."

☀️ Big Story of the Day
Quebec elects the Parti Québécois, a party that wants to leave Canada, but only a minority government

The Parti Québécois (PQ) won the most seats in last night's Quebec election and will form a minority government (it has the most seats but not more than half, so it needs other parties' votes to pass laws), according to CBC News projections. Its leader, Paul St-Pierre Plamondon, becomes premier. He promises a third referendum (a yes/no vote on independence) during his first term, but says he will wait until Donald Trump leaves office in January 2029. The PQ won only three seats in 2022. Political scientist Daniel Béland says support for independence is still around 30%, low by historical standards, and the result lands as a new national-unity test for Prime Minister Carney with an Alberta independence vote also coming (Al Jazeera). Related coverage: CBC key takeaways.

Video-worthy because: it is the result of yesterday's election-day story, and everyone asks "so is Quebec leaving?" Honest caveat: I could not open the CBC page itself (blocked), so exact seat counts are not in this brief. I used the CBC headline/projection and the Al Jazeera article text.

TRREB's September numbers are out: GTA home sales 5,040 (down 9% from last year), average price $1,006,409 (down 5.1%), and new listings 16,500 (down 14.4%). Buyers are waiting and sellers are too (TRREB release via GlobeNewswire).

🧠 Why it matters (to you)

Buyer: sales are down 9% and the average price is under last year, but the drop in new listings means sellers are not rushing, so do not expect a fire sale. TRREB says many households still plan to buy in the coming months. Newcomer: Quebec's result does not change your mortgage, visa or lease this week. A referendum is a separate vote and the PQ leader himself puts it after January 2029. First-time buyer: if you are saving for a first home, an FHSA (First Home Savings Account) only starts building contribution room once the account is open, so opening one before Dec 31 matters even with $0 in it, per Alex Applegate below.

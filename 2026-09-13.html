☀️ Big Story of the Day
Jean Chrétien, 92, brought a hockey arena to its feet: "we bow to no one."

On Friday, former Prime Minister Jean Chrétien delivered a fiery, deeply personal speech at the 25th-anniversary 9/11 commemoration in Gander, Newfoundland — the small town famous for taking in 38 stranded flights the day of the attacks. Chrétien was the only speaker who directly addressed the current tension between Canada and the US, telling the crowd "we embrace our friends and we help them in their moment of need, but we bow to no one," and warning Americans never to mistake Canadian kindness for weakness (CBC News, The Globe and Mail).

Why it's video-worthy: this isn't a real estate story, and it doesn't need to be — a 92-year-old former PM getting a standing ovation for refusing to bow, right in the middle of a trade war, is the kind of clip that travels on its own. For an audience of newcomers who know exactly what it's like to be the smaller country standing up to a much bigger neighbour, this one lands differently.

🎯 The One Thing
Toronto home prices just had their sharpest monthly drop of all of 2026 — and unsold inventory hasn't been this high since 2008.

TRREB's August numbers (released Sept 3, still the freshest official data) showed the average GTA home price slip 0.9% in a single month to $925,900 — 4.5% below last August and 27.7% below the March 2022 peak (Better Dwelling). Toronto realtor Zhen Liang posted her own breakdown of the exact same numbers today, framing it as a genuine turning point for buyers (YouTube, Sept 13) — a real news story and a creator independently landing on it the same weekend.

🧭 Why These Made the Cut · Fresh vs. Recent

Why these beat the other candidates: the price-drop story wins the lead slot because it's the single clearest wallet-and-decision story available — a real, verifiable data point (not a forecast) that changes what a buyer or seller should do this month, reinforced by a creator covering the identical numbers today. 🧠 Why It Matters (To You)

If you're buying this fall: August gave you real leverage — prices are down, inventory is the highest since 2008, and the sales-to-listings ratio is deep in buyer's-market territory. That doesn't mean lowball every seller, but it means you can negotiate harder than a year ago. If you're selling: price honestly from day one — the market is punishing anyone who tests a 2022-era number. If you're watching the trade war for your renovation or construction budget: Trump's "fairly soon" comment is a signal worth watching, not a reason to change plans — Canada already turned down one offer, and there's still no timeline. If you're shopping for a condo: don't just compare monthly fees — ask for the reserve fund study, because an under-funded building is a future five-figure bill waiting to happen.

Hook: "Nobody tells you this before you buy a condo — your monthly fee isn't the bill that gets you."

Body: "A story blew up this week about condo owners getting hit with special levies — extra one-time bills, on top of their regular fees — sometimes for fifty thousand dollars or more, to pay for decades of deferred repairs: roofs, underground pipes, elevators. In British Columbia, new rules just closed a loophole that let buildings skip proper reserve-fund studies for years, and now those bills are finally coming due. Ontario has its own version of this: condo corporations here are required to run a reserve fund study and top up their reserve fund regularly, but plenty of older buildings still under-fund it, especially ones built in the 80s and 90s that are now hitting major-repair age. Before you buy any condo here, ask for the status certificate and actually read the reserve fund study — not just the monthly fee. A low fee on an under-funded building is a future bill with your name on it. I've seen buyers, especially newcomers who didn't know to ask, get blindsided by exactly this."

☀️ Big Story of the Day
Canada and Ukraine just signed a 100-year partnership — and it could make it easier for your family to visit.

On Thursday in Calgary, Prime Minister Mark Carney and President Volodymyr Zelenskyy signed the 100 Year Partnership Declaration, elevating Canada-Ukraine ties into what's called a "Comprehensive Strategic Partnership" — basically a long-term commitment covering defence, trade, reconstruction, energy and more (PM's Office — Joint Statement). Canada is extending Operation UNIFIER (the military training mission for Ukraine's forces) through 2029, and building a new defence-industrial partnership to jointly produce drones and equipment. But the line that matters most for this audience is buried near the bottom: Canada will start a process to add Ukraine to the conditional electronic travel authorization (eTA) system as part of a new migration and mobility partnership (NOW Toronto). An eTA is the light-touch, online travel permission that citizens of many visa-exempt countries use instead of applying for a full visa — if Ukraine gets added, it would make visiting Canada meaningfully faster and simpler for Ukrainian citizens than the current visa process.

Why it's video-worthy: most of yesterday's coverage was about Zelenskyy's itinerary — planes, cities, photo ops. This is the actual outcome, signed and on paper, and it's the first genuinely new detail in this whole visit that could change something concrete for Ukrainian families trying to bring relatives to Canada.

🎯 The One Thing
Oil prices just dropped 3.6% in one morning — after the same spike that made your gas more expensive this week.

Brent crude, the global oil benchmark, fell 3.58% Friday morning to $103.78 a barrel, and U.S. crude (WTI) fell 2.17% to $99.23 — both pulling back sharply after Brent touched about $108 on Thursday, its highest point since May (CNBC). Even with Friday's drop, Brent is still on track to finish the week up roughly 8-9%, the first time it's closed a week above $100 since mid-May — this was a real spike, not a one-day blip. The whipsaw is being driven by the escalating U.S.-Iran conflict: Houthi rebels linked to Iran captured a Yemeni port city near a key Red Sea shipping route this week, and Saudi Arabia's oil output has fallen to its lowest level since 1990. Analysts told CNBC the bigger question is whether this supply squeeze is temporary or structural — and that further spikes "cannot be ruled out."

If you drive or budget monthly: the oil swing this week shows how fast and how far these prices can move on geopolitics alone — don't assume this week's high gas price is now permanent, but don't assume it's over either; pad your budget either way.

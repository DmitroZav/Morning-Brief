☀️ Big Story of the Day
Canada's economy just grew at its fastest pace in three years — and the recession scare from May is officially cancelled.

Statistics Canada released its second-quarter GDP numbers this morning. GDP (gross domestic product) is just the total value of everything Canada produces and sells — when it's rising, more money is moving through the economy and more jobs are being created; when it shrinks for two quarters in a row, that's technically a recession. The economy grew at an annualized 3.3% in Q2 (April–June), the fastest pace in more than three years, driven by a rebound in auto exports and the first rise in business investment in over a year. Just as important: StatCan also revised its first-quarter number. Back in May, it reported the economy had shrunk slightly, which sparked real recession talk. That number has now been revised UP to a small gain — so there was no contraction at all (The Canadian Press, via CKOM, Aug 28; BNN Bloomberg).

Why it's video-worthy: this is completely fresh economic data, released today, that flips a fear the whole country has been sitting with since May — and it directly changes the "is a rate cut coming" conversation. That's Idea 1.

🎯 The One Thing
Buried in the same report: growth already stalled to 0.0% in July — so the "best quarter in 3 years" might already be behind us.

StatCan's preliminary estimate for July — the first month of Q3 — shows growth flat at 0.0%, after June came in at +0.3% (investingLive, Aug 28 data release). That's the real number to watch: it's the first month fully inside the window where the new 50% US tariffs (live since Aug 22) start showing up in the data. Meanwhile, the Bank of Canada's next rate decision is Wednesday, September 2 (Bank of Canada, 2026 schedule) — a strong Q2 print gives the Bank zero reason to cut, so if you've been waiting on rates to drop because "the economy's struggling," that story just got weaker again. But don't get comfortable either: a flat July means that could flip fast.

🧠 Why It Matters (To You)

Anyone with a mortgage coming up for renewal or a fixed rate to lock: a strong economy gives the Bank of Canada no reason to cut rates on September 2. If your plan has been "wait for rates to drop because the economy's weak," that argument is weaker today than it was yesterday — but a flat July means it could reverse again by the next print.

First-time buyer who's been scared off by recession talk: the contraction StatCan reported back in May never actually happened — it's been revised away. That fear shouldn't be part of your decision anymore. What should be: your own budget, and the fact that growth already slowed to zero in July.

Renter or newcomer watching where people are moving: more people left Toronto than moved into it from elsewhere in Canada last year (see Idea 2 below) — even as the broader economy grows. That's a real tension worth understanding before you decide where to plant roots.

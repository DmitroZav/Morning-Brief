☀️ Big Story of the Day
The 2026 mortgage renewal wave finally arrives — and it's not the disaster everyone promised

For two years the headlines screamed about a "renewal cliff": hundreds of thousands of Canadians who locked in rock-bottom rates in 2020–21 having to renew at scary new rates. Well, it's here — 2026 is one of the biggest renewal years ever — and the reality is calmer than the fear. A renewal is simply the day your mortgage term ends and you re-sign at today's rate. The good news landing this week: roughly half of everyone renewing in 2026 will see their payment stay flat or actually drop, and in Toronto the average monthly payment on a benchmark home is projected to fall for the first time since 2020. Rates today (~3.94% fixed) are simply not that far above the 2021 deals for a lot of people.

But there's an honest catch, and it's the part the cheerful headlines skip. The Bank of Canada's own Financial Stability Report flags that about 9% of Toronto-area borrowers wouldn't be able to refinance their mortgage at renewal — their income no longer stretches to today's rules. And fresh data this morning shows bank mortgage delinquencies (people falling behind on payments) still creeping up. So it's a split screen: most people are fine or better off, a small but real group is squeezed hard.

Why it's video-worthy: everyone else did the "cliff panic" a year ago. You get to be the one who says the honest, nuanced version in plain words: "here's how to tell in 30 seconds whether your renewal helps you or hurts you." Useful, calm, and it's the exact question millions are quietly stressing about right now.

🎯 The One Thing
For the first time since 2020, the typical Toronto mortgage payment is set to fall at renewal — but a rising slice of owners still can't requalify. Know which side you're on before your term ends.

Plain version: if your mortgage renews this year, don't assume the worst. Half of Canadians renewing in 2026 pay the same or less, and Toronto payments are actually easing. The danger isn't the rate — it's getting caught flat-footed. Shop your renewal early, get a broker to run your real number, and if you're in the squeezed 9% who can't requalify, you want to know months ahead, not weeks. This is idea 1 below.

🧠 Why It Matters (To You)

If your mortgage renews this year: breathe. There's a good chance your payment stays flat or drops — Toronto payments are easing for the first time since 2020. But don't coast: start shopping your renewal 4–6 months early, get a broker to run your actual number, and don't just auto-sign the bank's first offer. If money's tight, find out now whether you can requalify, because the squeezed ~9% who can't need runway to make a plan.

If you're a first-time buyer or newcomer: the market is still soft and prices are below last year, so affordability is quietly the best it's been in a few years. Rates aren't falling fast, but they're not spiking either. This is a "get your real number and shop calmly" window, not a "panic-buy or panic-wait" one.

If you're a renter: the same soft market that has some owners stuck is handing you leverage. Empty condos mean landlords are competing for tenants and rents have cooled. If your lease is up, shop and negotiate instead of auto-renewing — most tenants don't realize the power shifted their way (see idea 3).

EU OFFERS CANADA "ASSOCIATE MEMBER" STATUS · NEW HOME STARTS STUCK FLAT · A SELLER'S-LOSS STORY DMYTRO KNOWS FIRSTHAND
☀️ Big Story of the Day
The EU just offered Canada something it has never offered any country before.

At her big annual "State of the Union" speech in Strasbourg, France today, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen told Canada's Prime Minister Mark Carney — the first foreign leader ever invited to that speech — that she wants to work with him on making Canada the European Union's first-ever "associate member." That status doesn't exist yet in the EU's rulebook; both sides would have to invent it, building on the existing Canada-EU trade deal (CETA) with a broader "Alliance for the Future" covering manufacturing, defence, energy, and AI (Al Jazeera, CNBC, Bloomberg).

Why it's video-worthy: this is the story every major outlet in the world is running today, and it's Canada's clearest answer yet to the US trade war — a real alternative to depending on one trading partner for 70% of exports. Even non-news-followers will hear about this one.

🎯 The One Thing
Canada barely built any more new homes in August than in July — and that's a big part of why rent isn't getting cheaper.

CMHC (Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation) released August's housing starts data this morning. "Housing starts" means new homes that actually broke ground. The number: 229,046 at a seasonally adjusted annual pace, essentially flat against July's 229,360 — and actual homes started in August were down about 2% from a year earlier. The 6-month moving average also slipped 1.3% from July (CP24/CMHC).

🧠 Why It Matters (To You)

If you care about the bigger picture: a closer Canada-EU relationship isn't going to change your mortgage tomorrow, but a Canadian economy that's less dependent on one trading partner's mood is a more stable economy long-term — and that stability eventually shows up in job security and how confident people feel buying a home. If you're renting, especially as a newcomer: today's flat CMHC construction numbers are the quiet reason rent keeps being expensive even in months when home prices cool off — supply just isn't catching up to how many people need a place to live. If you're thinking about buying your first home: Ilan Portnoi's video below is a reminder — from someone who's lived it — that markets move in both directions, and it's worth hearing the honest version before you sign anything.

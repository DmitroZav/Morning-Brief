☀️ Big Story of the Day
Trump says Canada is "destroying itself" over immigration — and on the same day, Carney admits he's studied the risk of a US invasion

On Wednesday, President Trump said Canada is "destroying itself" by letting in "millions" of immigrants "unchecked" — even though the government's own numbers show immigration levels have actually been going DOWN this year, not up. Canadian ministers called the claim "bogus" almost immediately (CBC, CP24). PM Carney didn't take the bait publicly — he spent the day promoting "nations coming together" instead (CTV News).

But in a separate New York Times interview released the same day, Carney revealed something much bigger: he has personally studied the possibility of Trump ordering a US-led military action against Canada, calling it an "extreme tail risk" — and saying, "That's not a base case, but it would be irresponsible not to [prepare]... just risk management" (CBC, CP24).

Why it's video-worthy: this is the single biggest story of the day even for people who don't follow Canadian politics — a sitting US president attacking Canada's immigration policy the same day our own PM admits he's gamed out what a US invasion would look like. And for anyone who moved to Canada from somewhere else, Trump's comment lands personally.

🎯 The One Thing
The number that actually sets your mortgage rate just had its biggest one-day jump in weeks — and it's not the Bank of Canada's fault this time.

Canada's 5-year government bond yield — what lenders actually price fixed mortgages off, not the Bank of Canada's overnight rate — jumped to 3.69% today, up 0.14 percentage points in a single session, its highest level in weeks (TradingEconomics, Bank of Canada). It's part of a broader global bond selloff — even the 10-year benchmark climbed to 3.86% — driven by inflation still sitting at 3% (above target), oil prices staying elevated on Middle East tension, and a more hawkish US Federal Reserve (Yahoo Finance). This is exactly the risk Bank of Canada Governor Tiff Macklem flagged two days ago in Halifax — that inflation could run hot enough to force a rate HIKE instead of the cut everyone's been waiting for (Bank of Canada). Today's jump is the first real sign that warning is showing up in the number that actually moves your renewal quote.

🧠 Why It Matters (To You)

If you're renewing or shopping for a mortgage in the next few months, today's bond yield jump is the first real evidence rates could move the "wrong" way — get pre-approved or lock in sooner rather than later, and don't assume a cut is coming just because everyone's been saying so.

If you're a renter — especially outside the downtown core — you may actually be catching a break: rent is falling fastest exactly where the trade war is hitting factory jobs hardest. Just don't expect that relief in Toronto itself any time soon.

And if you moved here from another country, or you're the parent of someone who did: today's headlines about "millions" and immigration are loud, but the actual data — plus the fact that your Prime Minister spent the day on UN diplomacy, not border crackdowns — tells a calmer story than the noise suggests.

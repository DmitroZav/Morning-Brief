☀️ Big Story of the Day
Quebec could elect a separatist government on October 5 — the polls are the closest they've been in years.

Quebec's provincial election campaign is now in its second week, with voting day set for October 5, 2026 (Élections Québec). The separatist Parti Québécois is leading or tied atop most polls — one recent survey has them at 28-31%, a few points ahead of the Quebec Liberal Party, with the governing CAQ trailing in third after voter anger over the cost of living (U.S. News, Aug 27). On day four of the campaign Sunday, party leaders focused on affordability — Québec solidaire proposed a rental registry and a crackdown on renovictions, the Conservatives pitched tax cuts, and the PQ doubled down on tightening immigration and extending French-language rules (Bill 101) to CEGEPs and to children of temporary immigrants (CBC News, Aug 30).

Why it's video-worthy: this is the kind of story that reaches people who don't follow housing or finance news at all — "a Canadian province might vote to leave the country" is a headline that travels. For a newcomer audience especially, it's a chance to talk honestly about what political and economic uncertainty actually does (and doesn't) do to your plans, without the fear-mongering.

🎯 The One Thing
Every single economist surveyed says the Bank of Canada holds rates Wednesday — and your fixed mortgage rate is still going up anyway.

The Bank of Canada makes its next rate announcement this Wednesday, September 2. A Reuters poll of all 35 economists surveyed — a rare unanimous result — expects the overnight rate to stay at 2.25%, with no change expected for at least another year, through Q3 2027 (Reuters via Investing.com, Aug 28). Normally that would be the whole story — a hold means nothing changes. Except fixed mortgage rates are rising anyway, because they don't follow the Bank of Canada's overnight rate; they follow the 5-year Government of Canada bond yield, which is being pushed toward a 12-month high (~3.36%) by a global bond selloff tied to surging U.S. government debt (which just passed US$40 trillion) and Middle East tensions. Lenders have raised fixed rates 10 to 15 basis points in the last two weeks, some by as much as 20 (Canadian Mortgage Trends, Aug 21, still moving as of a fresh Aug 31 mortgage-broker update). Two completely different mechanisms, moving in opposite directions on the same week — that's the part almost nobody explains clearly.

🧠 Why It Matters (To You)

Anyone shopping for a mortgage right now: don't wait for Wednesday expecting relief — the Bank of Canada holding steady doesn't freeze your fixed rate, because fixed rates already moved before the meeting even happened. If a lender offers you anything under 4.19% on a 5-year fixed today, brokers are calling that a good number, not one to negotiate down further.

Variable-rate holders and anyone renewing soon: variable rates haven't moved and aren't expected to for at least a year given the unanimous hold forecast — the gap between fixed and variable is widening again, which changes the fixed-vs-variable math compared to a month ago.

Newcomers and anyone watching the news out of Quebec: a genuinely close separatist-leaning election is unsettling to hear about, especially if you chose Canada for its stability. It's worth understanding calmly rather than reacting to headlines — Quebec has voted on this question twice before (1980, 1995) and stayed in Canada both times; a provincial election isn't a referendum, even with the PQ favoured to win seats.

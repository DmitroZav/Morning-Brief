☀️ Big Story of the Day
Ottawa just quietly gave every driver in Canada their money back — again.

The federal government paused the 10-cent-per-litre excise tax on gasoline back in April, when the war in Iran sent oil prices spiking. That pause was supposed to expire right after Labour Day — meaning gas was about to get 10 cents a litre more expensive at the pump starting next week. This morning, Finance Minister François-Philippe Champagne confirmed Ottawa is extending it: the tax stays paused, no reversal (CJME/The Canadian Press, Sept 2). The Conservatives had been pushing to extend the pause to the end of the year and add GST relief on top (they say that combo would cut pump prices by 25 cents a litre); Ontario Premier Doug Ford welcomed the extension and is now publicly asking Ottawa to make it permanent.

Why it's video-worthy: this is money-in-your-pocket news that needs zero explanation — everyone who drives, Ubers, or gets groceries delivered feels gas prices. It's also a rare "government gives you something" story in the middle of a year that's mostly been about tariffs taking things away, and it plays into a live political fight (three parties all claiming credit) that's easy to summarize in 30 seconds.

🎯 The One Thing
The Bank of Canada held rates today for the 7th straight time — but for the first time all year, its own economists say the next move could go either direction.

At 9:45 AM ET today, the Bank of Canada kept its key interest rate at 2.25%, exactly as expected (CJME/The Canadian Press, Sept 2). What's different this time: inflation is running hot at 3% because of oil prices tied to the Iran war, which normally would push the Bank to hike. But Canada's trade war with the US — 50% tariffs on $20B of our goods, with our own retaliation landing September 8 — is dragging on growth, which normally would push the Bank to cut. Economists are calling it a "stagflationary shock": both pressures at once, cancelling each other out into a hold (Canadian Mortgage Trends/Bloomberg, Sept 1). In a Bloomberg survey of economists, 63% now expect the Bank's next move to actually be a hike in the first half of 2027 — not the cut most people assume is coming. If you're holding a variable-rate mortgage or HELOC assuming rates only go one way from here, today's decision is a signal to stop assuming.

Today's angle is different: it's the actual decision, not a preview, and the takeaway is about direction-uncertainty (hike or cut both live) rather than the fixed/variable gap. The federal gas tax has never been a story in this brief before — today is the first time.

🧠 Why It Matters (To You)

If you drive: the gas tax pause staying in place is real, ongoing money back every single week — not a one-time rebate. No forms, no waiting. If you have a variable mortgage or HELOC: today's "could go either way" framing from the Bank of Canada means don't assume your payment is about to drop — budget as if it could just as easily rise. If you're renting and weighing whether to buy: the math isn't as simple as "rent is cheaper" — see Idea 3 below for the exact number that trips most renters up. If you're a newcomer: both stories are useful proof points that Canada's cost-of-living fights (gas, rates) are genuinely two-sided right now — not doom, not relief, just uncertainty you can plan around.

☀️ Big Story of the Day
The 35% tariff wall is no longer a warning — it's live as of this morning

Yesterday it was "starting tomorrow." Today it's real. As of August 1, the US officially raised its tariff — a tax on imported goods — on Canadian products that don't qualify under the CUSMA trade deal from 25% to 35%. Trump signed the order Thursday, ahead of the deadline. Energy and potash stay at a lower 10%, and CUSMA-compliant goods are still exempt.

Prime Minister Mark Carney says he's "disappointed" but struck a deliberately calm tone: even with this hike, Canada still faces among the lowest average tariff rates of any US trading partner, precisely because most of our trade moves under CUSMA. His bigger worry is the sector-specific tariffs already hitting lumber, steel, aluminum and autos — and a second wave of 50% tariffs on furniture, cement, lumber and dairy set for August 19.

Why it's video-worthy: the news cycle is screaming "35% TARIFF." Your job is the honest, calm-the-panic version: yes it's real, no it's not the apocalypse, and here's the one part that actually touches your rent and your future home — the building materials. That measured, wallet-first take is exactly what your audience isn't getting from the fear headlines.

🎯 The One Thing
The tariffs are live and fixed mortgage rates just climbed back above 4% — the "wait for cheaper borrowing" bet keeps getting weaker, not stronger.

Two things moved together. The bond market (where the government borrows money) got jittery — the 5-year Government of Canada bond yield ticked up to about 3.26%. That yield is what drives FIXED mortgage rates, so the best insured 5-year fixed just crossed back over 4% (4.04%), and most lenders' fixed rates are now above 4 again. Meanwhile a growing economy (Q2 tracked ~2.5%) plus sticky inflation plus fresh tariffs give the Bank of Canada fewer reasons to cut. For you: if you've been waiting on the sidelines betting rates will tumble, that bet is aging badly. Don't plan your purchase around a rate cut that keeps not showing up.

🧠 Why It Matters (To You)

If you're a first-time buyer or newcomer: stop waiting for a rate cut to rescue you. Fixed rates just went the wrong way (back above 4%), and the Bank of Canada has fewer reasons to cut with the economy growing again. The leverage you actually have right now is on price and choice — condos are down 9.4% from last year, GTA average prices are still below last year, and sellers are negotiating. Buy on the math that exists today, not the rate you're hoping for.

If you're a renter: the tariff headline doesn't change your rent this month, but the chain matters. If the Aug 19 wave taxes lumber and cement, building gets more expensive, fewer homes get built, and tighter supply eventually pushes rents up. Meanwhile the condo glut still works in your favour today — landlords are competing for tenants. If your lease is up, shop and negotiate rather than auto-renew.

If you already own or you're renewing: fixed rates ticking back above 4% is a nudge to act. If you renew in the next few months, ask your lender for a rate hold now — most lock today's number free for 90–120 days. It's free insurance against the next uptick. And if you're stretched, know the vocabulary in idea 3: "power of sale" is rising in the 905, and the earlier you talk to your lender, the more options you keep. See idea 2 and idea 3.

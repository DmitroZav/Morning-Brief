☀️ Big Story of the Day
Canada is trying to convince the world to invest $1,000,000,000,000 here — starting this week.

Prime Minister Mark Carney will host Canada's first-ever Investment Summit in Toronto on September 14 and 15, personally pitching foreign investors alongside cabinet ministers, premiers and business leaders (Global News, CTV News). The goal: "catalyze" $1 trillion in investment into Canada over five years. Why does this matter? Carney himself has called weak business investment Canada's "Achilles heel" — years of underinvestment mean fewer new factories, slower housing construction, and fewer private-sector jobs outside government and healthcare. His pitch to investors is that Canada is a reliable supplier in an uncertain, tariff-heavy world.

Why it's video-worthy: a trillion-dollar number and a Prime Minister personally selling the country grabs attention on its own — and the honest, non-hype angle ("watch what actually gets signed, not just promised") is exactly the kind of grounded take this audience trusts.

🎯 The One Thing
Canadians' debt compared to their income just went DOWN, not up — the opposite of what the scary headlines say.

StatCan reported yesterday that household credit market debt as a share of disposable income fell to 176.4% in Q2 2026, down from 178.6% in Q1 — because incomes grew faster than debt did (BNN Bloomberg). Mortgage borrowing also slowed to $19.4 billion for the quarter — the weakest pace since early 2024 — meaning fewer people are stretching to buy right now. This is a genuinely under-covered good-news number sitting right next to scarier "record $2.68 trillion in debt" headlines that only tell half the story.

🧭 Why These Made the Cut · Fresh vs. Recent

Why these beat the other candidates: the affordability story stacks three independent data sources (StatCan's fresh debt-to-income print, the National Bank's record 10th-straight-quarter affordability streak, and RBC's condo-back-to-2019-levels data) with two creators — Vancouver Life and Steve Saretsky — independently posting about the same underlying data within hours of each other today, which is a strong signal this is worth explaining before it gets buried. The Investment Summit is the single biggest national economic story available and passes the big-story test on its own. Tariffs and TRREB/CREA data also sit out (next CREA release is Sept 15). 🧠 Why It Matters (To You)

If you're a first-time buyer or newcomer: the "it's impossible to buy now" story is genuinely getting weaker — condo affordability in Toronto is now better than 2019, and mortgage payments as a share of income have fallen from 90.9% to 68.3% in under two years. Still expensive, but not the same story as 2023. If you're carrying debt or worried about a renewal: the debt-to-income improvement is real, but it's a national average — if you're one of the roughly one-in-three new borrowers who picked variable, CMHC's warning below is about you specifically, not the country in general. If you just want to know where the economy is headed: the Investment Summit this week is worth watching not for the trillion-dollar headline number, but for what specific projects and companies actually sign on — that's what would turn into real jobs in your city.

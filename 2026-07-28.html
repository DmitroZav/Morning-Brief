☀️ Big Story of the Day
Canada's home prices are now falling faster than any other rich country — but that's not the whole story

Fresh data out yesterday (Better Dwelling, using Bank for International Settlements numbers) puts a hard fact on paper: out of the G7 — the seven biggest advanced economies (Canada, US, UK, France, Germany, Italy, Japan) — Canada's real home prices are dropping the fastest. They fell 2.1% in the last measured quarter and are down 5.5% from a year ago, while most of the other six were flat or rising. From the 2022 peak, Canada is down about 28% after inflation.

Here's the twist that keeps it honest: Canada also rose the most on the way up. From 2010 to the 2022 peak, our real home prices jumped almost 99% — far more than anyone else. So even after this correction, Canada is still the second-most-expensive-versus-history market in the G7. In plain words: the air is coming out of a balloon that was blown up bigger than anyone else's. That's a slow deflation, not a sudden pop.

Why it's video-worthy: the headline "Canada's housing is crashing hardest in the developed world" is scary and shareable — but the honest take ("we also rose the hardest, so this is a bubble letting out air, not the end of the world") is the trust-builder nobody else leads with. Fear gets the click; calm context keeps the follow.

🎯 The One Thing
Prices are softening across the board — but the drop is a slow bubble-deflation, not a crash, and it's still handing first-time buyers the most negotiating power in years.

Every signal this week points the same way: Canada's the fastest-faller in the G7, Toronto condos are down ~8% year over year, and new-build single-family prices printed a scary one-month drop (more on that measurement quirk below). But sales have risen three months in a row nationally, the market's technically "balanced," and rate hikes are off the table. So if you're buying your first place, this isn't a moment to panic — it's the softest, most negotiable market you've seen since the bidding-war years. Prices down, choice up, sellers willing to deal.

🧠 Why It Matters (To You)

If you're a first-time buyer or newcomer: "fastest-falling in the G7" sounds terrifying, but read it the other way — prices are coming to you. Condos are down ~8% from last year with tons of choice, and even the scariest new-build numbers point to sellers who'll negotiate. This is the most leverage a first-timer has had in years: you can ask for a lower price, a longer closing, or repairs. Just don't try to time the exact bottom — nobody rings a bell, and a slow deflation can drag on for a long time.

If you're a renter: the same soft condo market is on your side. A glut of unsold and unrented units means landlords are competing on price. If your lease is up, don't auto-renew — shop and negotiate. Rare moment where the tenant holds a card.

If you already own — especially if you bought near the peak: honest talk, seeing "down 28% from the peak" hurts. But two things: it's on paper unless you sell, and the market isn't collapsing — it's slowly letting air out of a historic bubble. If you don't have to sell, you generally don't have to lock in the loss. Renewing a mortgage? Plan around today's ~4% fixed, not a rescue cut that isn't coming before September.

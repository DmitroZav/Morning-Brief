☀️ Big Story of the Day
Ottawa and Alberta just teamed up to build homes — a Liberal PM and a Conservative premier on the same stage

This morning in Red Deer, Prime Minister Mark Carney stood next to Alberta Premier Danielle Smith — two politicians who agree on almost nothing — to announce a joint plan to speed up homebuilding in the province. That optics alone is a story: a federal Liberal and Alberta's Conservative premier shaking hands on housing.

The engine behind it is Build Canada Homes, the new federal housing agency Carney set up with about $13 billion to fund factory-built (modular) homes — houses assembled in a factory and trucked to the lot, like giant LEGO. The pitch: prefab can cut build time by up to 50% and cost by up to 20%. The first wave is 4,000 modular homes on six sites (Edmonton is one), with room to scale toward 45,000. Today's Alberta deal plugs the province into that pipeline.

Why it's video-worthy: "the government is finally building homes" is a feel-good headline everyone wants to believe. Your honest job is the follow-up nobody says out loud — 4,000 to 45,000 homes is a rounding error against a shortage measured in the millions, and modular is promising but won't touch your rent or purchase price this year. Hope plus a reality check is the trust combo.

🎯 The One Thing
The government made a big promise to build more homes — but the thing actually moving your money this week is fixed mortgage rates ticking UP, even though the Bank of Canada hasn't touched its rate since spring.

Two speeds today. The slow, headline-grabbing story: Ottawa and Alberta announcing factory-built homes — real, but years away from changing what you pay. The fast, wallet-level story: the 5-year government bond yield has been drifting up, and that quietly pushes FIXED mortgage rates higher. The best 5-year fixed is back around 3.94% and creeping. The Bank of Canada's own meeting notes drop this afternoon (1:30 ET) and should confirm it's parked for now. So if you're shopping for a mortgage, the "wait and it'll get cheaper" bet just got weaker.

🧠 Why It Matters (To You)

If you're a first-time buyer or newcomer: two things pull in opposite directions today. Good news — affordability is the best it's been in four years, and condos are almost back to pre-pandemic reach, so your dollar buys more than it has in a long time. The catch — fixed mortgage rates are creeping up, so the monthly payment side is quietly getting more expensive even as prices soften. Translation: the "wait forever for a lower rate" plan is losing its edge. If the numbers work for you now, they may not get much friendlier.

If you're a renter: don't get too excited about the government's "build more homes" announcement. Factory-built housing is a real idea, but the first few thousand units are years and provinces away from your neighbourhood. Your rent this year depends on today's soft condo market — a glut of unsold, unrented units means landlords are competing. If your lease is up, shop and negotiate instead of auto-renewing.

If you already own or you're renewing: the rate story is the one to watch. Fixed rates ticking up means if you're renewing in the next few months, lock in your rate hold early — most lenders will guarantee a rate for 90–120 days, and that free insurance is worth grabbing while rates drift the wrong way. Don't count on a rescue cut before September; the Bank of Canada is parked.

CONDO CORRECTION · TARIFF CLOCK: 23 DAYS
☀️ Big Story of the Day
The tariff countdown is under 4 weeks — and Carney is now dropping Canada's own tariffs to get a deal

Quick refresher: a tariff is a border tax the importer pays, and it almost always gets passed on to regular people as higher prices. The big date is August 19 — that's when a 50% U.S. tariff is set to hit a long list of Canadian goods (dairy, alcohol, building materials, electronics, and more) unless there's a deal. A smaller 10% tariff is already live now on goods that don't qualify under the CUSMA free-trade agreement.

The fresh move: to keep talks alive, Prime Minister Mark Carney has started removing Canada's own 25% counter-tariffs on billions of dollars of American products. He and Trump agreed to "intensify" negotiations. Carney's honest caveat is that a deal with zero tariffs is unlikely — so the realistic goal is a smaller, livable tariff, not a clean escape.

Why it's video-worthy: people are exhausted by tariff headlines, so don't do "here's what happened." Do the countdown: 23 days to Aug 19, here's the one thing that changes for your wallet, and here's what to actually do before then. A date on the calendar beats another opinion.

🎯 The One Thing
The condo correction is the loudest story in the market right now — Toronto's average condo is down 8.2% from a year ago to about $537,300, and Vancouver is bleeding too.

Every creator we track this weekend was on the same theme: condos are getting cheaper while houses hold up. In June, the average Toronto condo sold for roughly $537,300 — down 8.2% from a year earlier — even as overall GTA sales hit their highest monthly count in almost two years. That split matters: if you've been priced out of a house, the condo side of the market is quietly turning into a buyer's market, with lots of choice and sellers who need to deal. It's not a crash to celebrate — plenty of owners who bought pre-construction near the peak are hurting — but for a first-time buyer or newcomer, this is the most negotiating power condos have handed you in years.

🧠 Why It Matters (To You)

If you're a first-time buyer or newcomer: the condo side of the market is the most buyer-friendly it's been in years. Prices down 8% from last year, tons of listings, and sellers who often need to sell. That's leverage. You can ask for a lower price, a longer closing, or repairs — things that were impossible in the bidding-war years. Start with what's possible (a condo), not the dream house, and use this softness to get in.

If you're a renter: the same condo glut helping buyers is helping you. All those new units have to be filled, so Toronto landlords are competing on price and throwing in incentives. If your lease is up, don't just auto-renew — shop around and negotiate. This is a rare moment where the tenant has some power.

If you already own a condo you bought near the peak: honest talk — this is painful, and you're not alone. If you don't have to sell right now, you generally don't have to lock in the loss; the market isn't collapsing, just correcting. If you're renewing a mortgage, plan around today's ~4% fixed rate, not a rescue rate cut that likely isn't coming before September.

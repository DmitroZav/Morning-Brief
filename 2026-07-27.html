FIXED RATES TICKING UP · TARIFF CLOCK ~AUG 19
☀️ Big Story of the Day
The tariff clock is under four weeks — and this time markets are starting to react, not just headlines

The weekend brought no breakthrough in the Canada–U.S. trade talks. Prime Minister Mark Carney and Donald Trump agreed last week to "intensify" negotiations toward one comprehensive deal, but the threat still stands: a 50% U.S. tariff on a long list of Canadian goods — cement, lumber, dairy, liquor, furniture, even hockey gear — is set to land around August 19 unless there's an agreement. A 10% tariff is already hitting some goods now. (A tariff is a border tax the importer pays, and it usually gets passed on to regular people as higher prices.)

What's new this week: the pressure is showing up in money, not just microphones. Oil is jittery on Middle East tension, and Canada's government bond yields — the thing that quietly sets your fixed mortgage rate — have drifted up. So the trade story and your mortgage story are now the same story.

Why it's video-worthy: People are numb to "tariff" headlines. The fresh angle is the countdown plus the connection: "here's the date, and here's how it's already nudging your mortgage." That's a story a non-news-follower still cares about, because it ends at their wallet.

🎯 The One Thing
Fixed mortgage rates just ticked UP — not down. The 5-year bond yield climbed to about 3.2% on oil and Middle East tension, and lenders nudged fixed rates ~0.05% higher.

Here's the plain-language chain. Fixed mortgage rates don't follow the Bank of Canada — they follow the 5-year Government of Canada bond yield (the interest the government pays to borrow money for five years). When oil spikes on war worries, investors expect inflation to stick around longer, so they demand a higher yield to lend — and that yield has moved from about 3.13% in mid-July to roughly 3.2% now. Lenders followed: the best insured 5-year fixed has crept up around 0.05%, sitting near 3.94%. It's a small move, but the direction matters. If you've been telling yourself "I'll just wait, rates are about to drop," the fixed side just went the other way.

🧠 Why It Matters (To You)

If you're shopping for a mortgage or renewing: the fixed vs. variable math shifted a little this week. Fixed rates edged up while variable sat still. If you were waiting for fixed rates to fall before locking in, this is a nudge that the timing isn't in your control — a war headline on the other side of the world can move your rate. Get a rate hold now if you're close to buying; a hold locks today's rate for 90–120 days at no cost, so a rise can't catch you.

If you're a buyer eyeing a condo: prices are soft and sellers are sitting. That's leverage for you — but don't rush. Presale buyers in some markets are walking away from deals (more in Creator Watch), which tells you the "buy anything, it only goes up" era is over. Buy something you'd be happy to hold for years, not a flip.

If you're new to Canada: two things to watch. Fixed rates ticking up means budget on today's rate, not a hoped-for cut. And August 19 matters — the industries that hire newcomers first (construction, manufacturing) are the ones most exposed to these tariffs. Keep a cash cushion and don't over-stretch right before a possible shock.

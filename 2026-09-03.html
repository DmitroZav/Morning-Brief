☀️ Big Story of the Day
The face on your $20 bill just changed for the first time in your life.

This morning the Bank of Canada unveiled the design of the new $20 bank note — and for the first time ever, it features King Charles III instead of Queen Elizabeth II, who died in September 2022 after 70 years on the throne (980 CJME/The Canadian Press, Sept 3). The new bill is green, vertical (same modern layout as the Viola Desmond $10 from 2018), with King Charles on the front and the Canadian National Vimy Memorial on the back (Bank of Canada, Sept 3). It won't actually start circulating until early 2027, but today is the reveal. The reigning monarch has appeared on Canadian bills since 1935 — so this is only the second face-change most people alive today have ever seen.

Why it's video-worthy: zero financial literacy required, genuinely interesting for a newcomer building a mental map of Canadian symbols and history, and it's a nice break from tariffs and interest rates — pure "did you know" content that still gets someone to stop scrolling.

🎯 The One Thing
Toronto's home listings just fell 14% in a year — and the board that tracks the market says that's exactly the kind of squeeze that pushes prices back up.

TRREB's August 2026 Market Watch report landed at 5 AM this morning (TRREB/GlobeNewswire, Sept 3). GTA REALTORS® reported 5,057 home sales in August — down 2.1% from a year ago. But the real headline is new listings: only 12,075 homes came onto the market, down 14.1% year-over-year — a much sharper drop than sales. Fewer homes for sale plus roughly the same number of buyers means less choice, and TRREB's own economist points out that's the exact setup that tends to push prices higher, not lower. The average price, at $993,410, is still down 2.7% year-over-year, and the benchmark price index (a more accurate "typical home" measure) is down 4.5% — so today's market is still cheaper than a year ago. But the direction of travel — sellers pulling back, not buyers rushing in — is the thing to watch. TRREB's CEO also used the report to push governments to cut zoning barriers, taxes, and approval delays that are keeping new supply from being built in the first place (Full TRREB Market Watch PDF, August 2026).

🧠 Why It Matters (To You)

If you're a first-time buyer waiting for prices to keep dropping: today's TRREB numbers are the clearest sign yet that the "wait and it gets cheaper" bet is getting riskier — sellers are pulling listings faster than buyers are disappearing. If you're stretching to afford a home: nearly 6 in 10 new CMHC-insured mortgages now run 30 years instead of 25 — a real tool, but it means paying more interest over time, so know what you're trading off. If you're confused by conflicting headlines: you're not wrong to be — a big bank calling this a "recovery" and a realtor calling it a "death spiral" can both be using real numbers; see Idea 3 for how to read past the headline. If you're just here to understand Canada better: today's new $20 bill is a small, fun piece of the country's story worth knowing, newcomer or not.

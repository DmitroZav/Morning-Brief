☀️ Big Story of the Day
Canada just climbed out of recession — and tomorrow a 35% tariff wall lands on our exports

Two big things collided this morning. First, Statistics Canada released the May GDP numbers plus an early "flash" read on the spring quarter. GDP — gross domestic product, basically the total value of everything the country produces — grew a tiny 0.1% in May, slower than April's 0.5%, but the early tracking for the full April–June quarter is a solid ~2.5% growth (annualized). That matters because Canada had just gone through two straight quarters of shrinking (a "technical recession"). So on paper, we're out.

Second, the timing couldn't be worse. Starting tomorrow, August 1, the US raises its tariff — a tax on imported goods — on Canadian products that don't qualify under the CUSMA trade deal from 25% to 35%. Energy and potash stay at a lower 10%, and CUSMA-compliant goods are still exempt. A second wave of 50% tariffs on furniture, cement, lumber, dairy and more is set for August 19. Prime Minister Carney called it a direct violation of the trade agreement.

Why it's video-worthy: "We left the recession for exactly one day" is a hook that writes itself. The honest, calm-the-panic version — we're growing again, but a trade fight starting tomorrow could tax the very building materials that make homes cheaper — is exactly the plain-language, wallet-first story your audience isn't getting anywhere else.

🎯 The One Thing
The economy is technically growing again (~2.5% in Q2) — but tomorrow's 35% tariffs threaten the recovery, and if building materials get taxed, the cost of new homes goes the wrong way.

Here's the chain in plain English. Growth is back, which normally eases pressure and could eventually help rates settle. But a big chunk of the new 50% tariff wave (Aug 19) hits construction materials — lumber, cement, furniture. When it costs more to build, builders either raise prices or stop building. Canada's housing starts already fell 6% in June and are down 13% from a year ago. Fewer new homes plus pricier materials is the opposite of what a country with an affordability crisis needs. For you: don't read "we're out of recession" as "all clear" — the next month is where it actually gets decided.

🧠 Why It Matters (To You)

If you're a first-time buyer or newcomer: the softest parts of the market — condos (−9.4% from last year) and brand-new homes (worst month on record) — are where you have the most power right now. Motivated sellers, room to negotiate, and on new builds a possible HST rebate worth up to $130,000 on homes under $1M. The risk on the horizon: if tariffs push up the cost of building materials, new-build prices could firm back up later this year. Today's soft new-home market may be a window, not a permanent state. See idea 2.

If you're a renter: the "we're out of recession" headline doesn't change your rent tomorrow, but it does lower the odds of a quick rate cut — so don't count on falling rates to suddenly make buying cheap. Meanwhile the condo glut still works in your favour: landlords are competing for tenants. If your lease is up, shop and negotiate rather than auto-renew.

If you already own or you're renewing: a growing economy plus sticky 3.2% inflation plus new tariffs that could nudge prices up = the Bank of Canada has fewer reasons to cut and one more reason to wait. Fixed rates aren't falling. If you renew in the next few months, ask your lender for a rate hold now — most lock today's number free for 90–120 days. Free insurance while the picture stays cloudy. See idea 3.

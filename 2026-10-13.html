Script (30-60s)

Hook: Owning a typical Canadian home takes 52.8% of the average household's income. That number just stopped getting better.

Body: RBC tracks how much of a household's income goes to owning a home: mortgage, property tax, utilities. In Q2 it fell by 0.4 points to 52.8%. Good news, right? But that's the smallest improvement in almost a year. And RBC says all of it came from incomes growing, not from prices or rates falling. Fixed mortgage rates are already creeping up because bond yields rose, and RBC expects Bank of Canada hikes to squeeze people on variable mortgages next year. Their call: a modest loss of affordability as 2027 rolls in. Translation for a first-time buyer: the easy gains are over. If you're ready and approved, waiting for 'cheaper' is no longer a free bet. Script (30-60s)

Hook: That listing you liked just disappeared. It probably didn't sell. It was terminated.

Body: A listing can leave the market three ways: sold, expired, or terminated, which means the seller or agent pulled it before the contract ran out. In September, GTA sales were 5,040, down 9% from last year, and the average price was $1,006,409, down 5.1% (TRREB). Sellers who can't get their number often pull the listing and wait. For you as a buyer, a pulled or relisted house is a signal: the seller may have already learned their price was too high. Check how long it was listed and whether the price dropped. Ask your agent for the history, then come in with a clean, fair offer. Karl Nielsen at Toronto Property Finders covered the trend today; I'm taking it from the buyer's side, and I'm only using TRREB's own numbers here.

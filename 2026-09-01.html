☀️ Big Story of the Day
Overnight, Mark Carney's Liberals went from a minority to a majority government — and flipped a Quebec seat while doing it.

Three federal byelections were held Monday, and the Liberals won all three: they held Beaches-East York in Toronto (about 55% of the vote) and North Vancouver-Capilano in B.C., and — the real headline — they flipped Chicoutimi-Le Fjord, Quebec away from Pierre Poilievre's Conservatives, who fell to a distant third place (CP24, Sept 1). That gives the Liberals 173 of 343 seats in the House of Commons — a solid majority, meaning Carney's government can now govern without the constant risk of losing a confidence vote, all the way to the next scheduled election in 2029 (CBC News, live coverage).

Why it's video-worthy: this is the kind of headline that reaches people who never watch a housing or finance video — "the government just got a majority overnight" is a story anyone with a passport cares about. And for newcomers specifically, a government that can't be knocked over by a snap election means more predictable rules on immigration, housing policy and the trade fight for the next few years, not less.

🎯 The One Thing
Today is the day the money bar for studying in Canada went up — and almost nobody explains where that number comes from.

Effective today, September 1, 2026, anyone submitting a new study permit application must prove they have at least $23,448 in savings for living expenses — up from $22,895 (Moving2Canada, Aug 2026). That's on top of first-year tuition and return travel costs. IRCC updates this "proof of funds" number every year to track the actual cost of living in Canada — it's not a random hike, but it is a real and rising bar (IRCC — financial support requirements, official government page). If you applied yesterday, the old number still applies to you. If you apply from today onward, this is the new floor — and it's a detail a lot of families budgeting for a move here never see coming until it's too late.

🧠 Why It Matters (To You)

Anyone watching Canadian politics wondering "does this affect me": a majority government means fewer surprise elections and more room for multi-year policy — immigration levels, housing programs, and the tariff fight are all things this government can now commit to for longer without worrying about being toppled. Good for planning, whatever you think of the party in charge.

Families budgeting for a study permit application: the $553 jump in required savings is small in isolation, but it's part of a pattern — this number has gone up every September for years. Build in a buffer above whatever number you see online, because by the time you apply it may already be out of date.

Anyone with a mortgage thinking about breaking it early (to catch a better rate, sell, or refinance): the penalty math is not simple interest — it depends on your lender's specific formula, and guessing wrong can cost thousands more than expected. Ask for the exact payout number in writing before you decide anything.

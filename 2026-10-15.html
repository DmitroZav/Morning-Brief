☀️ Big Story of the Day
Ottawa says Stelco's U.S. owner could be forced to sell Canadian assets, and the 500 Hamilton layoffs are going ahead anyway

Industry Minister Melanie Joly said "everything is on the table", including forced sale of assets or financial penalties, because Cleveland-Cliffs (the U.S. company that owns Stelco) is cutting up to 500 jobs at its Hamilton plant. Cliffs says it did not break the promises it made to Ottawa in 2024 and blames low steel prices and U.S. tariffs. Layoff notices are going to union members and the cold mill and coating departments are being idled. Sources: BNN Bloomberg, Oct 14, CBC: Ottawa weighs legal action, CBC: Stelco says layoffs still happening. Why it is video-worthy: a foreign owner vs the federal government over local jobs is the clearest tariff-war story in Ontario right now. Held as a headline only, no video today, because Stelco already had videos on Oct 11 and Oct 13.

🧠 Why it matters (to you)

If you are a first-time buyer or newcomer: cheaper lumber duties are good news on paper, but Toronto started only 156 condos in six months, so the real problem is that nobody is building, not the price of wood. If you drive, heat a home or run a small business, the oil boom is good for the country and bad for your wallet at the same time, and TD warns pump prices may not fall even if crude does. Script (30-60s)

Hook: Pick Hook 1 or 2 on camera.

Body: Most Canadian homes are built with wood frames, and a lot of our lumber is sold to the U.S. The U.S. charges extra taxes on it. In August 2025 the total hit 45%. After the latest U.S. review it is almost 35%, made of three pieces: 14.11% countervailing duty (extra tax the U.S. says offsets Canadian government help), 10.64% anti-dumping duty (extra tax for selling too cheap), and a 10% tariff on national-security grounds. The B.C. lumber council still calls it a significant burden. Now the part that matters for you: Toronto started only 156 condos in the first half of this year versus about 7,000 a year on average (CMHC data). So even with cheaper wood, there is almost nothing being started. My honest take: this will not change what you pay this year. Builders need buyers, permits and financing before wood even matters.

Why this touches your mortgage: since the war began, the 5-year government bond yield has climbed from about 2.6% to above 3.6%, and fixed mortgage rates follow it.

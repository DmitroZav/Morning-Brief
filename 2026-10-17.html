☀️ Big Story of the Day
Albertans vote Monday on whether to start down the road to leaving Canada, and the Prime Minister is campaigning in the province to stop it

On Monday, Oct 19, Albertans vote in a provincial referendum (a public yes/no vote) on whether the province should hold a later vote on independence. About 1.02 million ballots, roughly one third of eligible voters, were already cast by Oct 16. Polls cited by Time put “stay in Canada” around 70–75%. Carney called a vote against Canada “a dangerous bluff” that would bring years of uncertainty. Sources: BNN Bloomberg, Oct 17, Time, Oct 16 (also reports Trump again floated Canada as the “51st state”). Why it is video-worthy: even people who ignore news will want a plain-English explainer before the result lands. I have no data yet on any housing or dollar effect, so the video says that honestly.

🎯 The One Thing
CMHC (the federal housing agency) says Canada started homes at a yearly pace of 248,896 in September, up about 3% from August, but actual urban starts were 19,984, down about 11% from a year ago (WRE News on CMHC, Oct 16).

🧠 Why it matters (to you)

Alberta: nothing changes in your mortgage on Tuesday. What people watch is uncertainty, because banks, employers and investors dislike not knowing the rules. If you are a newcomer, it is worth knowing how Canada works when it argues with itself, and the vote is about politics, not your PR. Housing starts: Canada needs more homes to ease prices and rents, and September started 11% fewer real homes than last year. A first-time buyer should read that as: supply is not coming fast, so do not count on a price crash alone to fix affordability. If you rent, new supply is what eventually pulls rents down.

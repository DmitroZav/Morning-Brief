☀️ Big Story of the Day
Ontario just put a real financial-literacy test between your teenager and their high school diploma.

Starting this school year (2026-2027), Ontario Grade 10 students must complete a brand-new Financial Literacy Graduation Requirement built into their mandatory Career Studies course — modules developed by TVO for the Ministry of Education, and students need 70% or higher to pass it, worth 5% of the course's final grade (CBC News). It's part of a bigger back-to-school shake-up: mandatory written final exams are back for Grades 9-12 (20% of the mark in Grades 9/10), report cards now grade attendance, and kindergarten got a revised curriculum (CBC News).

Why it's video-worthy: this reaches every newcomer parent with a teenager in an Ontario school, has zero connection to the trade-war news cycle everyone's been covering for two weeks, and "my kid needs to pass a money test to graduate" is the kind of surprising, shareable fact that travels outside the real estate niche entirely.

🎯 The One Thing
Canada's tariffs actually went live at 12:01 this morning — and Carney told the country flat out there's real pain coming.

At one minute past midnight, Canada's retaliatory tariffs on $27.6 billion of US goods took effect: 15%, 25%, or 50% depending on the product, with steel and aluminum duties doubling from 25% to 50% to match the US (Department of Finance Canada). Hours later, Prime Minister Carney released a national video address saying Canada's pivot away from relying on the US "will come at a cost" but that the alternative is worse, and calling Trump's tariffs "designed to harm some Canadians more than others" (Lethbridge Herald; 980 CJME). This is no longer a countdown — steel and aluminum are core inputs for framing, siding, and fixtures, so any renovation or new-build quote you got last month may already be out of date.

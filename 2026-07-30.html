☀️ Big Story of the Day
The Bank of Canada published its private meeting notes — and the people who decide your mortgage rate can't agree if the economy is actually recovering

Yesterday afternoon (1:30 ET) the Bank of Canada released its "summary of deliberations" — basically the behind-closed-doors notes from the meeting where they held the rate at 2.25% two weeks ago. Normally these are dry. This one wasn't: it shows the governing council is split. Some members think Canada is climbing out of its stalled patch; others aren't convinced the recovery is real, and a few are worried that people are starting to expect higher inflation again — which can become a self-fulfilling problem.

In plain language: the group in charge of the country's interest rates looked at the same economy and walked out disagreeing about where it's headed. "Inflation expectations" just means what regular people and businesses assume prices will do next — and if everyone braces for higher prices, they tend to actually rise, which makes the Bank reluctant to cut rates.

Why it's video-worthy: the honest headline nobody says is "the experts are guessing too." For anyone waiting for a rate cut to swoop in and rescue their mortgage, this is the reality check: even the Bank of Canada isn't sure a cut is coming. That's a trust-building, calm-the-panic story — and Friday's May GDP number (out July 31) is the next clue that could tip the argument either way.

🎯 The One Thing
Toronto's new-condo market just posted its first sales jump in three years (+52%) — but read the fine print: it's a fat tax rebate and big investors doing the buying, not regular first-time buyers coming back.

New condo sales across the Toronto–Hamilton area rose 52% from a year ago in the spring quarter — the first annual gain since 2023. Sounds like the bottom is in. But almost all of that jump came from bulk sales to investment groups snapping up finished, unsold buildings, plus a new HST rebate worth up to $130,000 on new homes under $1 million pulling some buyers off the fence. Sales are still 86% below the normal 10-year pace, and the supply of new projects is "thinning quickly" because builders stopped launching. For you, the takeaway is mixed and useful: prices are soft and there's real money on the table via that rebate — but don't mistake investor activity for a full recovery.

🧠 Why It Matters (To You)

If you're a first-time buyer or newcomer: the softest, cheapest part of the market — condos — is also where the government is handing out the most money. New-build condos under $1M can qualify for an HST rebate worth up to $130,000, and condo prices are down more than 9% from a year ago. That's a rare combo: lower price AND a cash rebate. The catch is it only applies to new/pre-construction, and you have to actually claim it — most people I meet don't know it exists. If a new condo fits your plan, this is a real moment. See idea 1.

If you're a renter: the condo glut is still your friend. Thousands of finished, unsold units mean landlords are competing for tenants — so if your lease is up, shop and negotiate instead of auto-renewing. But note the other half of the story: builders have stopped launching new projects, so a few years out there'll be fewer new rentals coming. Today's oversupply is a renter's window that won't stay open forever.

If you already own or you're renewing: stop waiting for the Bank of Canada to rescue you. Its own meeting notes show the council is divided and nervous about inflation — a rate cut before September is not the safe bet people assume. Meanwhile fixed rates are creeping up with bond yields. If you renew in the next few months, grab a rate hold now — most lenders lock today's number free for 90–120 days. That's free insurance while rates drift the wrong way.

☀️ Big Story of the Day
It's crunch week: 6 days until a 50% U.S. tariff wall hits Canada — and there's still no deal.

This is the single biggest thing hanging over the Canadian economy right now, and the clock is loud. Washington has threatened a 50% tariff — a 50% tax at the U.S. border — on roughly $20 billion of Canadian goods (think autos, dairy, aluminum, lumber, alcohol) starting August 19, and it would hit even goods that were supposed to be protected under the CUSMA trade deal (BNN Bloomberg). Canada's trade minister Dominic LeBlanc met his U.S. counterpart in Washington on Tuesday — the third meeting in three weeks — and talks are now happening daily right up to the deadline (KFGO / Reuters).

As of this morning the two sides have swapped written positions but have not reached an agreement. Canadian negotiators have privately warned the Americans that if the 50% tariff actually lands, it could halt the trade talks entirely and force Prime Minister Carney into retaliation (Globe and Mail). So it's genuinely deal-or-no-deal, and we find out within a week.

Why it's video-worthy: most people think "tariffs" is boring political news that doesn't touch them. It absolutely does — it moves prices, jobs, and how nervous buyers feel about signing for a house. Explaining the countdown in plain language, with real "what's in it for you" stakes, is exactly the lane no realtor is filling. That's idea 1.

🎯 The One Thing
The number of Canadians 90+ days behind on their mortgage just hit the highest level in over a decade — about 14,061 households, more than double the 2022 low — and it's climbing at a pace usually only seen in a recession.

Plain version: more people are falling behind on payments as ultra-cheap pandemic mortgages renew at ~4%. It's still a small share of all mortgages (0.29%), so it's not a crash — but the trend is real, and if your renewal is coming, now is the time to make a plan, not panic. Sources: Better Dwelling — arrears hit recession levels; Canadian Mortgage Professional. That's idea 2 below.

🧠 Why It Matters (To You)

If you're a buyer or seller waiting for the market to "settle": the tariff deadline is the fog. A 50% tariff wall could slow the economy, threaten jobs, and make buyers freeze — or a last-minute deal could lift the cloud fast. Nobody signs a mortgage feeling brave when the headlines are about a trade war. Watch Aug 19: it's the closest thing to a real market signal you'll get this month (KFGO/Reuters). That's idea 1.

If your mortgage renews soon: the arrears record is a nudge, not a scare. More people are falling behind because pandemic mortgages at ~1.5% are renewing at ~4%, and the count is rising fast (Better Dwelling). The move isn't to panic — it's to run your renewal numbers 4–6 months early, shop lenders, and know your options before the letter arrives. That's idea 2.

If you're wondering "is it finally time to buy": here's the honest paradox. RBC says affordability just improved to the best level in about 4 years — but it's still as stretched as the early-1990s bubble (Better Dwelling / RBC). Translation: things got a little less impossible, not actually easy. Don't let "best in 4 years" trick you into overstretching — run your real budget. That's idea 3.

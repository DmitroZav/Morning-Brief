☀️ Big Story of the Day
The 50% tariff clock is now under three weeks — and the talks just got serious

It's a quiet long weekend here (today is the Civic Holiday, so Canadian markets and banks are closed), but the calendar is loud. On August 19 — 16 days from today — a second, bigger wave of US tariffs is set to hit: 50% on roughly $20 billion of Canadian goods. A tariff is just a tax on things crossing the border, and this batch lands on lumber, plywood, cement, furniture, clothing, appliances and more. Unlike the first round, this one is written to apply even to goods that normally qualify under our free-trade deal (CUSMA), and it has no end date built in.

The one piece of good news: Prime Minister Mark Carney and President Trump have agreed to speed up trade talks, giving negotiators a roughly 30-day window to land some kind of deal before the deadline bites. Energy, potash, steel, aluminum, autos and critical minerals are carved out of this particular wave, so Canada's biggest exports are shielded. Meanwhile the retaliation is still on the shelf: most provinces (all but Alberta and Saskatchewan) are still keeping American beer, wine and spirits off their liquor shelves.

Why it's video-worthy: everyone else is doing "35% tariff panic" content that's already a week stale. You get to be the guy with the calendar: here's the ONE date that matters (Aug 19), here's the ONE thing on the list that touches your future home (lumber and cement), and here's the deal that could still cancel the whole thing. Calm, specific, useful — that's the post people send to their family.

🎯 The One Thing
Fixed at ~3.94% or variable at ~3.50%? A giant wave of mortgage renewals is landing this year, and this is the one choice that decides your payment for the next 5 years.

Here's the plain version. A "renewal" is when your mortgage term ends and you sign a new rate — hundreds of thousands of Canadians are hitting that moment this year after locking in during the cheap-money era. Right now the two doors are close together: the best 5-year fixed is around 3.94% (locked, never moves) and a 5-year variable is around 3.50% (cheaper today, but it moves with the Bank of Canada). The Bank has been parked at 2.25% since October and isn't rushing to cut, while fixed rates are being nudged up by the bond market. So the "obvious" cheaper option isn't obvious at all — and picking on autopilot, or just re-signing whatever your bank emails you, is how people quietly overpay by thousands. This is today's clearest wallet decision, and it's idea 1 below.

🧠 Why It Matters (To You)

If you're renewing a mortgage this year: this is your month to pay attention. Fixed (~3.94%) and variable (~3.50%) are unusually close, and the Bank of Canada is not going to ride in with a quick rescue cut — the economy is growing again. Don't just re-sign the renewal letter your bank sends; that's rarely their best rate. Shop it, or have a broker shop it, and decide fixed-vs-variable on your own nerves and math, not on hope.

If you're a first-time buyer or newcomer: the condo market is the softest, most buyer-friendly corner in years. Thousands of finished units are sitting empty, prices are still below 2024, and sellers are competing for you. Your leverage today is on price and choice — negotiate hard, ask for incentives, take your time. Just remember the flip side: condo building has stalled to a 30-year low, so this window of oversupply won't stay open forever.

If you're a renter: the empty-condo glut is quietly on your side — landlords are competing for tenants and rents have cooled. If your lease is up, shop and negotiate instead of auto-renewing. Longer term, watch Aug 19: if tariffs tax lumber and cement, building gets pricier, fewer homes get built, and tighter supply eventually nudges rents back up.

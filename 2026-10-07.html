☀️ Big Story of the Day
Ontario lost a net 29,713 people to other provinces in 12 months. Alberta was the top destination.

Statistics Canada's Q2 2026 data, covered by Better Dwelling on Oct 5, shows Ontario lost a net 7,063 residents to other provinces in the second quarter and 29,713 over 12 months. Interprovincial migration means Canadians moving between provinces, not immigrants arriving. Alberta gained 5,893 in Q2 and 26,376 over the year; B.C. gained 2,756 in Q2. Better Dwelling calls Ontario's problem structural. Alberta also votes Oct 19 on whether to ask about leaving Canada, so where people move matters.

Video-worthy because: it answers "where should I live?" with a real number. Honest caveat: the article is two days old and I did not open the original StatCan table. Nothing bigger broke in the last 24h in my sources.

TRREB's September report: 67.9% of the 1,316 GTA condos that sold went for under $600,000, and the average condo price was $605,257, down 7.7% (STOREYS, Oct 6).

Hook: The federal foreign buyer ban ends January 1, 2027. Here is what Ottawa is thinking about next.

Body: The ban stops non-Canadians from buying residential property in cities and larger towns. Citizens and permanent residents are not the target, but check your exact status before buying. It expires January 1, 2027. MPA reports the Carney government is considering an Australian-style approach: foreign buyers could buy new construction and vacant land, but not existing homes, to push money into building. Foreign buyers were only 1.1% of B.C. sales, down from 3% in 2017, while Canada builds about 259,000 homes a year against the 430,000 to 480,000 it needs. Nothing is decided yet. This is a plan being discussed, not a law.

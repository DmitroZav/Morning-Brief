☀️ Big Story of the Day
Toronto picks a mayor on Oct 26, and the two front-runners want opposite things for your housing costs

Incumbent Olivia Chow wants 65,000 rent-controlled homes by 2030, keeps development-charge breaks for small projects and backs higher taxes on vacant and luxury homes. Challenger Brad Bradford wants the city to skip the municipal land transfer tax (the city's own tax you pay when you buy, on top of the Ontario one) on the first $1.1 million of a home price, and opposes the extra taxes on vacant and luxury homes (Storeys comparison, CTV on the tax pledge, Sept 29). Chow warns the cut would force service cuts (CTV). Storeys also reports fewer than 1 in 6 Torontonians can afford to buy and the city is 32% of the way to its 285,000-homes goal. Why it is video-worthy: it is 12 days to the vote, and almost nobody explains what each plan means for a renter or first-time buyer. Note: the land-transfer pledge itself dates from Sept 29; today's new hook is the side-by-side comparison.

🎯 The One Thing
Builders took out permits for $13.6B of buildings in August, up 8.8%, and the whole gain was apartments and condos; houses were flat (CMT / StatCan, BNN Bloomberg).

Script (30-60s)

Hook: Quick move-in or pre-construction? One is a finished home. The other is a promise.

Body: A QMI, quick move-in, is a new home the builder has finished or nearly finished and still has to sell. Pre-construction means you buy before it is built and wait years. Question one: what is the total closing cost, including development charges, levies and any HST you are expected to pay? Question two: what happens if the closing date moves, and what is my deposit protection? Question three: what is the market price for similar resale homes nearby today? Prices in Toronto are about 28 percent below the 2022 peak, so a builder's price from two years ago can be above today's resale. Check the HST rebate rules on canada.ca for your situation before you count on it. Statistics Canada says August permits were up 8.8 percent, almost all apartments and condos, so more new supply is coming.

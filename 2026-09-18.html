☀️ Big Story of the Day
The US just raised interest rates for the first time since 2023 — and your Canadian dollar already felt it.

On Wednesday, the US Federal Reserve voted 12-0 to raise its benchmark rate a quarter point to 3.75%–4.00%, its first hike in three years, aimed at fighting inflation that's stayed "elevated" even with oil prices spiking (CNBC, CNN Business). Sixteen of eighteen Fed officials think at least one more hike is coming this year. Markets reacted within hours: the Canadian dollar slid to its weakest level since early August — a "6-week low" — because when US rates climb and Canadian rates hold still, money tends to chase the US dollar (Kitco, FXStreet).

Why it's video-worthy: this is a decision made entirely in Washington that most Canadians will only ever feel as "why does everything imported suddenly cost more" — a clean, satisfying explainer connecting a foreign headline directly to a Canadian wallet, and a genuinely new story for this brief (first time covering a Fed decision, not a BoC one).

🎯 The One Thing
A weaker loonie makes anything American pricier right now — but oddly, it did NOT make your mortgage more expensive this week.

A weaker Canadian dollar means cross-border shopping, US flights and anything priced in US dollars costs a little more starting now, and a persistently weak loonie can quietly add to inflation here at home (Global News, The Motley Fool Canada). But here's the twist: Canada's own 5-year government bond yield — the one that actually sets your FIXED mortgage rate — eased to about 3.53% this week, down from roughly 3.68%, pulling the opposite direction of the US news (TradingEconomics). So the scary headline and your actual mortgage math aren't the same story right now.

🧭 Why These Made the Cut · Fresh vs. Recent

Why these beat the other candidates: the Fed's first hike in three years takes the lead because it's the single most consequential global economic event in months, with a clean, provable link straight to a Canadian's wallet through the loonie. Both the Fed-rate-hike angle and Ontario's rental-law changes are brand new to this brief — never covered before.

🧠 Why It Matters (To You)

If you're watching your dollar: a weaker loonie after the Fed hike means anything priced in US dollars — a cross-border Amazon order, a Florida trip, imported groceries — got a little pricier overnight, and a weak loonie that stays weak can feed back into inflation here too. If you have a mortgage renewing or you're about to buy: don't panic off the "Fed just hiked" headline alone — Canada's own bond yield actually eased this week, so your fixed rate isn't automatically about to jump; get a real quote instead of guessing from headlines. If you're renting or about to rent in the GTA, especially as a newcomer: the deadline that actually affects you personally isn't in Washington, it's Monday — Ontario's eviction and hearing rules change Sept 21, so know your rights (and keep your rent receipts) before then.

Hook: Starting Monday, it gets faster to evict a tenant in Ontario — here's exactly what's changing.

Body: September 21 is when the second wave of Ontario's rental-law changes (Bill 60 and Bill 97) kicks in. The headline change: the N4 eviction notice for unpaid rent shrinks from 14 days to 7 days. "Persistent late payment" now has an actual legal definition — paying more than 7 days late, three times in six months. If a tenant wants to raise certain defences at a hearing, they now have to pay 50% of the claimed rent arrears at least 7 days before that hearing. And there's new protection for tenants against bad-faith "landlord's family is moving in" evictions — landlords now have to actually move in within 60 days or face penalties up to $50,000 plus up to 12 months' rent in compensation. If you rent in the GTA, especially if you're new here, read the actual notice you get — don't assume the old timelines still apply after Monday.

☀️ Big Story of the Day
Toronto's July numbers just landed — and the buyer's market is quietly closing

TRREB (the Toronto Regional Real Estate Board — the group that tracks every home sale in the GTA) dropped its July report this morning, and it flips the story of the last two years. For ages the headline was "tons of homes for sale, buyers in charge." Now look what happened in July: sales were basically flat (5,995, down just 0.9% from last year) but new listings fell off a cliff — down 17.8% from a year ago. Fewer homes for sale + steady buyers = the market is tightening. TRREB's own words: buyers "may find there is less room to negotiate moving forward," and prices "could start to level off."

Here's the honest part, because it's not a boom. The average price is still $1,003,956, down 4.5% from a year ago, and the benchmark home (the "typical" place, stripped of the pricey outliers) is $933,800, down 4.6%. So prices are still soft — but the pressure that's been dragging them down is easing, because sellers are simply pulling their homes off the market instead of dumping them cheap. Analyst Daniel Foch calls it "the sellers who didn't show up" — owners who won't accept a lowball just stop listing, and that props prices up.

Why it's video-worthy: everyone in your feed is still saying "it's a buyer's market, take your time." You get to be the one holding up this morning's data and saying, calmly, "actually the window is starting to close — here's how to tell if you should move now or keep waiting." Timely, useful, and slightly against the crowd.

🎯 The One Thing
Toronto sellers pulled listings hard in July (−17.8%) while buyers stayed put — so the "endless choice, name your price" buyer's market is quietly tightening, even though prices are still down 4.5% year-over-year.

Plain version: for two years buyers had all the leverage — piles of listings, lots of room to negotiate. This morning's data shows that starting to shift. Sellers who won't take a low price are just not listing, so choice is shrinking. Prices haven't turned up yet, but the discount you can squeeze out is getting smaller. If you've been sitting on the fence waiting for the "perfect" bottom, this is the report that says: your negotiating power is strongest right now, not necessarily in six months. This is idea 1 below.

🧠 Why It Matters (To You)

If you're a first-time buyer or newcomer: your negotiating power is quietly shrinking. For two years you could take your time and lowball; this morning's data shows listings drying up and choice narrowing. Prices are still down 4.5% year-over-year, so it's not too late — but the "sit and wait forever" plan is getting riskier. Get your real budget from a broker now and be ready to move on the right place, because the discounts are smaller than they were in spring.

If you own a condo: the softest corner of the market is still the cheapest condos — they're the hardest to sell right now because so many investor units are competing. That's painful if you're selling, but if you're a newcomer buying your first place, it's where the real deals and the most negotiating room still live (see idea 2).

If you're waiting for a crash: the honest read is a floor forming, not a boom. Jobs and growth data have surprised to the upside, and sellers are pulling listings rather than slashing prices. That combination is how corrections quietly end. You don't have to panic-buy — but "wait for it to get way cheaper" is now a weaker bet than it was three months ago (see idea 3).

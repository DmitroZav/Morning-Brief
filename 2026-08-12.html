☀️ Big Story of the Day
Toronto landlords are now the ones competing — two months free rent, gift cards, free parking, just to fill units.

For years the renter had zero leverage: line up, bid over asking, take it or leave it. That has flipped. Toronto-area landlords are openly advertising perks to lure tenants — two months of free rent, free parking, and gift cards for groceries or transit — because too many units are sitting empty (CBC News). Roughly two-thirds of newer rental buildings are offering an incentive right now, and the most common one is two months free (Storeys / CMHC mid-year update).

The reason: a record wave of new purpose-built rentals opened at the same time immigration slowed, so there are more empty apartments than people to fill them. The vacancy rate in newer GTA-Hamilton buildings hit 5.4% — the highest since the early-pandemic ghost-town days of 2021 (Storeys / CMHC). Asking rents are down: an average one-bedroom is around $2,313, down 4.4% in a year, and once you count the free months, effective rents in the City of Toronto have dropped about 3.8% (CBC News).

Why it's video-worthy: this is the single most useful thing you can tell a newcomer this month — the exact people who felt powerless in the rental market last year can now negotiate. It's positive, it's specific, and no one in the "buy a house" lane is talking to renters like this.

🎯 The One Thing
The rental power has flipped to tenants: Toronto landlords are offering two months free rent, gift cards and free parking to fill a record number of empty units — the best position renters and newcomers have had in half a decade.

Plain version: if your lease is up or you're new to the city, you can now negotiate — ask for a free month, ask for parking thrown in, and don't take the first number. Sources: CBC News; Storeys — CMHC mid-year rental update. That's idea 1 below.

🧠 Why It Matters (To You)

If you rent or you just arrived: the ground shifted in your favour. Landlords are the ones competing now — two-thirds of newer buildings are dangling incentives and the most common offer is two months free (Storeys/CMHC). You can ask for things: a free month, free parking, a lower renewal instead of an increase. A year ago that was unthinkable. Use it (idea 1).

If you're about to get a mortgage: the fork in the road just moved. For the first time in about three years, the best variable rate (~3.40%) is cheaper than the best fixed (~3.94%) (Ratehub). Variable saves you money today but rides with the Bank of Canada, so it can climb; fixed costs a little more but locks your payment. Get pre-approved so the choice is real numbers (idea 2).

If you keep seeing "crash" and "$450K loss" headlines: take a breath. Some investors and pre-construction buyers are selling at real losses, and forced sales are climbing (Daniel Foch). But a scary headline about someone else's loss is not the same as a discount in your pocket — the average GTA home is still just over $1M (TRREB). Learn what's driving those losses so you spot a genuine deal instead of catching a falling knife (idea 3).
